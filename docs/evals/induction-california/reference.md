# Replacing a gas stove with induction in California in 2026

*Reference answer written independently with web research, sealed in git before Quorum answered.*

> Should a California homeowner replace a gas stove with induction in 2026? Costs, rebates, health evidence.

**Bottom line: Yes if your kitchen already has (or can cheaply get) a 240-volt circuit, or anyone at home has asthma:
induction removes the main indoor source of nitrogen dioxide and cooks better. If it would force a panel upgrade,
wait until your stove needs replacing anyway, and use a vented range hood in the meantime; the big state rebate is
fully reserved for now.**

## Key points

- **Health evidence: real exposure, debated size of harm.** Gas and propane stoves raise indoor nitrogen dioxide;
  a 2024 Stanford–Harvard study (Science Advances, measurements in 100+ kitchens) estimated about 50,000 current
  cases of US childhood asthma are attributable to gas-stove NO2, with small homes (under 800 sq ft) getting about
  4× the long-term exposure of large ones. Stanford also found gas stoves emit benzene, in some homes above health
  benchmarks (2022–2023). The size of the asthma link is contested: a 2024 meta-analysis (Puzzolo et al.) found a
  small, not statistically significant increase for children (OR 1.09, 95% CI 0.99–1.19). Ventilation reduces
  exposure a lot; induction eliminates combustion pollution entirely.
- **Upfront cost.** An induction range typically costs about $1,000–$3,000. The hidden cost is electrical: if there's
  no 240 V/40–50 A circuit at the stove, adding one runs roughly a few hundred to about $1,500+; if the panel is full,
  a panel upgrade can cost several thousand dollars. Alternatives avoid it: 120 V induction ranges with built-in
  batteries, or circuit-sharing devices. A plumber caps the gas line.
- **Rebates in 2026 are thin.** California's federal-funded HEEHRA program offered up to $840 for an electric or
  induction stove for income-qualified households, but single-family funding has been fully reserved since
  February 24, 2026, with new requests waitlisted. The federal 25C tax credit ended December 31, 2025 (it never
  covered stoves). Check local utility and community-choice programs (Switch Is On lists them); some Bay Area
  programs lend induction cooktops to try first.
- **Running cost is a wash.** Induction is far more efficient than gas (roughly 85% vs 30–40% of the energy reaching
  the pan), but California electricity is expensive, so the yearly cooking bill changes by tens of dollars at most.
- **Cooking.** Induction boils water faster than gas, gives precise low heat and a cooler kitchen; you need magnetic
  cookware (cast iron, most stainless steel) and lose flame cooking such as charring on the burner.

## Where the evidence is uncertain

- Whether gas stoves cause asthma, versus being associated with it, is still debated; exposure studies and
  population estimates point one way, and some meta-analyses find weak or non-significant effects.
- Rebate funding changes during the year; the HEEHRA waitlist could reopen, and Phase II money is not yet available.
- Electrical costs vary hugely by house; get a quote before committing.

## What this means in practice

1. Check for an existing 240 V outlet behind the stove and your panel's spare capacity (an electrician's load
   calculation).
2. If someone has asthma, a small home or poor ventilation, prioritize the switch; until then, run a hood that vents
   outside on high, or open a window, whenever you cook.
3. Look up current rebates by ZIP code, and join the HEEHRA waitlist if you qualify.
4. Try a $60–$100 portable induction burner first to learn the cookware and cooking differences.
