> Intermittent fasting vs. daily calorie restriction for weight loss: what does the evidence say?

*Answer · ended at the round limit (2 rounds)*

**Bottom line: A calorie deficit is the only proven driver of weight loss, so pick the method—intermittent fasting or continuous calorie restriction—that you can follow consistently for months.**

## Key points
- **Evidence of equivalence** – A 2021 NEJM trial and a 2022 Cochrane review found that when total calories were matched, IF and daily calorie restriction produced similar weight loss (≈5–7 % of baseline) and metabolic improvements, though specific metabolic benefits were not fully addressed. [2]
- **No clear extra benefit** – No large study has shown that IF confers greater weight‑loss than the same caloric deficit from CCR, though this does not conclusively cover all forms of IF. [3]
- **Hormonal hints** – It is uncertain whether IF improves insulin sensitivity and lowers insulin levels.
- **Adherence matters** – Drop‑out rates in IF trials have been reported as slightly lower in some studies, but the evidence is limited.

## Details
- **Safety** – IF is generally safe for healthy adults but should be avoided or modified in people with diabetes on glucose‑lowering meds, eating‑disorder histories, or certain medical conditions; consult a clinician if unsure. 
- **Starting point** – Estimate your maintenance calories and aim for a 500–750 kcal/day deficit to lose ~0.5–1 kg/month. 
- **Monitoring** – Weigh yourself weekly, track body‑composition changes, and adjust the plan if weight loss stalls. 
- **Flexibility** – You can switch between IF and CCR if one feels unsustainable; the key is to keep the deficit steady over time.
